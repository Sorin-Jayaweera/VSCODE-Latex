## A
We have the $E$ for $\ket{E,L,M}$ given to us, we just time evolve each portion.
$$
\begin{align}
\ket{\psi(t)} = \frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{-i}{\hbar}}\ket{1,0,0} + \frac{1}{\sqrt[]{ 2 } }e^{-\frac{2i}{\hbar} } \ket{2,1,1} + \frac{1}{2} e^{-\frac{2i}{\hbar} } \ket{2,1,0} 
\end{align}
$$

The Hamiltonian looks like
$$
\begin{align}
\hat{H}  & = \hat{H}_{\text{ hydrogen }} + \omega_{0} \hat{L}_{z} \\
\end{align}
$$
The Hamiltonian of the Hamilton has states $\ket{n,l,m}$, which is also an eigenstate of $\hat{L}_{z}$. 

This gives us the time evolution as
$$
\begin{align}
e^{-i \hat{H}_{\text{ hydrogen }} - \omega_{0} \hat{L}_{z} }
\end{align}
$$
$E_{n}$ for hydrogen is
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\mu z^{2}e^{4}}{2\hbar^{2}n^{2}}
\end{align}
$$

where 
$$
\begin{align}
E_{1} = \frac{-\mu e^{4}}{2\hbar^{2}} \\
E_{2} = \frac{-\mu e^{4}}{8\hbar^{2}}
\end{align}
$$

The eigenvalues for these are
$$
\begin{align} 
\ket{1,0,0}  & \text{ has } E= E_{1} \\
\ket{2,1,1} & \text{ has } E= E_{2} -\omega_{0}\hbar \\
\ket{2,1,0}  & \text{ has } E=E_{2}
\end{align}
$$


So the time evolution is
$$
\begin{align}
\psi(t) =  \frac{1}{2} e^{-i \frac{E_{1}}{\hbar}}\ket{1,0,0} + \frac{1}{\sqrt[]{ 2 } } e^{-i(\frac{E_{2}}{\hbar}+\omega_{0})\hbar }\ket{2,1,1} + \frac{1}{2}e^{-i(\frac{E_{2}}{\hbar})}
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
\left< E \right>    = \braket{ \psi|H | \psi }  & = \frac{1}{4}(E_{1}) + \frac{1}{2}(E_{2} + \omega_{0}\hbar) + \frac{1}{4}(E_{2})  \\
 & = -\frac{\mu e^{4}}{8\hbar^{2}}  -\frac{\mu e^{4}}{16\hbar^{2}} + \frac{\omega_{0}\hbar}{2} - \frac{\mu e^{4}}{32 \hbar^{2}} \\
 & = -\frac{\mu e^{4}}{\hbar^{2}} \left(  \frac{1}{8}-\frac{1}{16}-\frac{1}{32} \right) + \frac{\omega_{0}\hbar}{2} \\
 & = -\frac{\mu e^{4}}{\hbar^{2}} \left(  \frac{4}{32}-\frac{2}{32}-\frac{1}{32} \right) + \frac{\omega_{0}\hbar}{2} \\ \\
 & = \frac{\omega_{0}\hbar}{2}-\frac{1}{32} \frac{\mu e^{4}}{\hbar^{2}}
\end{align}
$$
