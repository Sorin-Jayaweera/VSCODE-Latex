We have four possible states for $n=2$
$$
\begin{align}
\ket{2,0,0} \\
\ket{2,1,-1}, \ket{2,1,0}, \ket{2,1,1}    
\end{align}
$$
each of which can have $S= \pm \frac{1}{2}$
We can find the energy of the perturbing Hamiltonian on each of those states.
![[Pasted image 20260430140518.png]]

We can build the matrix to see how this applies:
$$
\begin{align}
\bra{\psi} \hat{H}_{B} \ket{\psi}  = \frac{eB}{2m_{e} c} \braket{ \psi | \hat{L}_{z} + 2\hat{S}_{z} |\psi } 
\end{align}
$$

I'll call $\frac{eB}{2m_{e}c}=C$

We can write this out for every state

$$
\begin{align}
C(m_{l} + 2m_{s}  )
\end{align}
$$
For all spin $\frac{1}{2}$, that will be $m_{l}+1$, and all $-\frac{1}{2}$ it will be $m_{l}-1$.

We get
$$
\begin{align}
 & \ket{2,0,0, \frac{1}{2}}   & & = C \\
 & \ket{2,0,0, -\frac{1}{2}}   & & = -C  \\ 
 & \ket{2,1,-1, \frac{1}{2}}   &  & =0\\
& \ket{2,1,-1,- \frac{1}{2}}  &  & = -2C\\
 & \ket{2,1,0, \frac{1}{2}}   & & = C \\
 & \ket{2,1,0, - \frac{1}{2}}  &  & = -C \\ 
& \ket{2,1,1, \frac{1}{2}}   & & = 2C \\
 & \ket{2,1,1, - \frac{1}{2}}  &  & = 0 \\
\end{align}
$$
These are all the expectation values of energy, the diagonal elements of a matrix where the rows and columns are these as bras and kets, with the hamiltonian sandwhiched in between. These are how much each of the eigenstate's energies are changed, and linear combinations of those eigenkets will have the same changes applied. 

All of these have a root energy $E_{0}$, from which they are $\pm 0,1, \text{ or } 2 c$

## A
To have a total spin 0 state, the particles must have opposite spin. 
The two particles are not interacting and spin isn't in the Hamiltonian, so they'll occupy the regular ground states. 

The ground states of the SHO is a Gaussian, and both of them have the same Gaussian because they have the same Hamiltonian. Thus, the spatial ground state for each particle $x=1 \text{ and } x=2$. 

$$
\begin{align}
\braket{ r | 0}_{x}  =  \left( \frac{m\omega}{\pi \hbar } \right)^{\frac{3}{4}} e^{-m\omega \frac{r^{2}}{2\hbar}}
\end{align}
$$
The first excited state for each is the same as the $\ket{1}$ of the SHO:
$$
\begin{align}
\braket{ r | 1 }_{x} = \left( \frac{m\omega}{\pi \hbar} \right)^{\frac{1}{2}} \left[ \frac{4}{\pi} \left(  \frac{m\omega}{\hbar} \right)^{3} \right]^{\frac{1}{4}}r \sin\theta \cos \phi e^{-\mu \omega \frac{r^{2}}{2\hbar}}
\end{align}
$$

$E_{nx} = \left( n+\frac{3}{2} \right)\hbar \omega$

Both can be in their ground states (lowest energy) at the same time, since spin doesn't matter for any interaction (they have opposite spin, $\pm \frac{1}{2}$). Under exchange (because they are identical particles), we want them to not change, so the state must NOT be symmetric (fermions can't stack).

## B

If they are closer than some radius, then we have a square potential (it's a circle, a radial step function). We can act this new potential as a perturbation to the Hamiltonian - because it's a negative constant$(-v_{0})$, the energy of each particle ground state is just lowered by the constant when they are within $a$ of each other. 

The symmetric position means that under exchange of labeling, it's the same state - so the particles are in the same position. Yay FERMIONS!

We are doing a perturbation around the ground state of the SHO, $\ket{0}$, and vary the frequency. We don't know the eigenstates of the momentum because its a free particle, so let's only consider the whole thing for the variation.
(thanks Annika)
$$
\begin{align} \\
\text{ the perturbing hamiltonian for the position term is } \\
\bra{0} b\hat{x}^{4}\ket{0}  \\
\end{align}
$$
We can write this as the raising and lowering operators

We can't lower below the ground state, so we destroy any term ending in $\hat{a}$ or more lowering operators than raising in order, or any unequal numbers of raising and lowering.

$$
\begin{align}
\left( \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega} \right)^{2}b \braket{ 0|(\hat{a}+\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{4} | 0 } 
\end{align}
$$

If we have equal raising and lowering, this is
$$
\begin{align}
\hat{a}^{2}\hat{a}^{2\dagger} + \hat{a}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\hat{a}^{\dagger} + \hat{a}\hat{a}^{2\dagger}\hat{a}  + \hat{a}^{2\dagger} \hat{a}^{2}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a} + \hat{a}^{\dagger} \hat{a}^{2}\hat{a}^{\dagger} 

\end{align}
$$
If we get rid of the other invalid ones, leaving the terms
$$
\begin{align}
\hat{a}^{2}\hat{a}^{2\dagger } + \hat{a}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\hat{a}^{\dagger} 
\end{align}
$$
We now get
$$
\begin{align}
\left( \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega} \right)^{2} b* (2+1)  
\end{align}
$$
This gives the position part as
$$
\boxed{
\begin{align}
3 \left( \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega} \right)^{2}b
\end{align}
}
$$

We can do the same with the momentum portion
$$
\begin{align}
\hat{p}_{x} =  -i \sqrt[]{ \frac{m\omega \hbar}{2} } (\hat{a}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}) \\
\hat{p}^{2}_{x} =- \frac{m\omega \hbar}{2}(\hat{a}^{2}+\hat{a}^{2\dagger}- \hat{a}\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a})
\end{align}
$$

We have the same rules applying, so we only have $-\hat{a}\hat{a}^{\dagger}$

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\left< \hat{P}_{} x \right>^{2}}{2m}= \frac{m\omega \hbar}{4m}
\end{align}
$$

We combine these to get

$$
\begin{align}
E=3 \left( \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega} \right)^{2}b  +\frac{\omega \hbar}{4}
\end{align}
$$

We can minimize this with respect to $\omega$. Derive!

$$
\begin{align}
0  & = -\frac{3\hbar^{2}b}{4m^{2}} \frac{2} {\omega^{3}} + \frac{\hbar}{4} \\
\omega^{3} & =6\hbar \frac{b}{m^{2}} \\
\omega  & = \sqrt[3]{ 6\hbar \frac{b}{m^{2}} } 
\end{align}
$$

We can use this to find $E_{0}$:
$$
\begin{align}
E_{0} &= \frac{3b\hbar^{2}}{4m^{2}\omega^{2}} + \frac{\hbar\omega}{4} \\
&= \frac{3b\hbar^{2}}{4m^{2}} \left( \frac{m^{2}}{6\hbar b} \right)^{2/3} + \frac{\hbar}{4} \left(\frac{6\hbar b}{m^{2}}\right)^{1/3}
\end{align}

$$
This is going to be awful
$$
\begin{align}
 & \left( \frac{3}{4 \cdot 6^{\frac{2}{3}}} + \frac{6^{\frac{1}{3}}}{4} \right) b^{\frac{1}{3}} \frac{\hbar^{\frac{4}{3}}}{m^{\frac{2}{3}}} \\
\end{align}
$$
This is
$$
\begin{align}
1.081 b^\frac{1}{3}\left( \frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m} \right)^{\frac{2}{3}}
\end{align}
$$
The exact result is really close!

We have the Hamiltonian
$$
\begin{align}
\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{P}^{2}}{2\mu}+ \frac{1}{2} \mu\omega^{2} \hat{r}^{2}

We guess that $R(r)= Ne^{-br}$
We can determine $N$, the normalization constant
$$
\begin{align}
1 = \int_{0}^{\infty} r^{2} dr \left| R(r) \right| ^{2} = \left| N \right| ^{2} \int_{0}^{\infty} r^{2} dr e^{-2br} = \left| N \right| ^{2} \frac{1}{4b^{3}}

This gives 
$$
\begin{align}
N=2b^{3/2}
\end{align}
$$
and the trial wave function
$$
\begin{align}
R(r)Y_{0,0} = \frac{1}{\sqrt[]{ 4\pi } }R(r) = \frac{b^{3/2}}{\sqrt[]{ \pi }  }e^{-br} 
\end{align}
$$
We can get the expectation value as
$$
\begin{align}
 & \int d^{3}r \, \psi ^{*} \left[  \frac{-\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\left( \frac{ d ^{2}}{d r^{2} } + \frac{2}{r} \frac{ d }{d r }  \right) + \frac{1}{2}\mu \omega^{2}r^{2} \right]\psi
\end{align}
$$
This evaluates to
$$
\begin{align}
 E  & = 4b^{3} \int_{0}^{\infty} dr \left( - \frac{\hbar^{2}b^{2}}{2\mu}r^{2}+ \frac{\hbar^{2}b}{\mu }r+ \frac{1}{2 }\mu \omega^{2} r^{4} \right)e^{-2br} \\
 & = \frac{\hbar^{2}b^{2}}{2\mu} + 4b^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2}\mu \omega^{2}r^{4}e^{-2br}dr \\
 & = \frac{\hbar^{2}b^{2}}{2\mu} +\frac{3\mu \omega^{2}}{2b^{2}}
\end{align}
$$


We minimize the expectation value of the energy, 

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\hbar^{2}b}{\mu} - \frac{6\mu \omega^{2}}{2b^{3}} = 0
\end{align}
$$
$$
\begin{align}
b^{4} = \frac{3\mu^{2}\omega^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}
\end{align}
$$

We can plug this back in to find the energy
$$
\begin{align}
E & =\frac{\hbar^{2} }{2\mu} \frac{\sqrt[]{ 3 }\mu \omega}{\hbar} +\frac{3\mu \omega^{2}}{2} \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt[]{ 3 } \mu \omega} \\
 & = \frac{\sqrt[]{ 3 }}{2}  \hbar \omega+ \frac{3}{2\sqrt[]{ 3 } }\hbar \omega \\
 & = 1.73 \hbar \omega
\end{align}
$$

The exact form of the ground state energy is
$$
\begin{align}
E  & = \left( 2n_{r}+ l+\frac{3}{2} \right)\hbar \omega \\
 & = \frac{3}{2}\hbar \omega \\
 & = 1.5 \hbar \omega
\end{align}
$$
These two are off by $0.23$, but our approximation is close.
