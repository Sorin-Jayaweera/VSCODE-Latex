## 5.1 Two spin $\frac{1}{2}$ particles

We could be nice and label by two particles in the state
$$
\begin{align}
\ket{+z,+z } 
\end{align}
$$
Or mean and label in any basis, such as
$$
\begin{align}
\ket{+z,+x} 
\end{align}
$$
They're complete basis, so there is a mapping between them.
$$
\begin{align}
\ket{+x,+z } = \frac{1}{\sqrt[]{ 2 } } \ket{+z,+z} + \frac{1}{\sqrt[]{ 2 } }\ket{-z,+z}   
\end{align}
$$

We can also rotate particles. Rotators applying to different particles commute, since they don't depend on order (unless entanglement?).

We define a new operation instead of the regular inner product and outer product: The direct product
$$
\begin{align}
\ket{+z,-z} = \ket{+z}_{1} \otimes  \ket{-z} _{2} 
\end{align}
$$
Which we can simplify notationally as just $\ket{+z}_{1}\ket{-z}_{2}$.
