## 1.1: Stern Gerlach

Lets see why spin angular momentum of a subatomic particle is different from orbital angular momentum.

Magnetic Dipole Moment
$$
\begin{align}
\mu = \frac{IA}{c} = \left( \frac{q}{T} \right) \frac{\pi r^{2}}{c} = \frac{qvr}{2c} = \frac{q}{2mc}L
\end{align}
$$
where A is area of circle, $I$ is current, $q$ is charge, $T$ is orbital period $T= \frac{2\pi r}{\nu}$ and $L=m\nu r$ is the orbital angular momentum $I=\frac{q}{T}$.

Orbital spin $\mu= \frac{q}{2mc}L$.
Intrinsic spin $\mu= \frac{gq}{2mc}S$, where $g$ is a proportionality constant.

## 1.2: 4 experiments
If we have a stream of $\ket{+z}$ going through a $z$ oriented Stern Gerlach, we would only see $\ket{+z}$. If we feed that pure $\ket{+z}$ to an $x$ oriented SG, then we would see half $\ket{+x}$ and half $\ket{-x}$. Putting it through another SG in the z would give us... not pure $\ket{+z}$, but instead half each $\ket{\pm z}$.
$S_{z}$ and $S_{x}$ are incompatible observables.
