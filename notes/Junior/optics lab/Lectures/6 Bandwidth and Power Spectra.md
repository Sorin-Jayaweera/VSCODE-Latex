## Doppler Broadening

Atoms in a gaseous form with a thermal distribution of velocities. They are each pointing in different directions, with various velocity components along the direction being observed. There is a doppler shift due to this movement.
$$
\begin{align}
\omega\approx \omega_{0}\left( 1+ \frac{v_{z} }{c} \right)
\end{align}
$$
Each atom emits a wave crest every $\frac{2\pi}{\omega_{0}}$ in its own frame. 

The atom emits a wave crest every $\frac{2\pi}{\omega \sqrt[]{ 1- \frac{v^{2}}{c^{2}} }}$ in the observer's frame

If the atom is moving towards $v_{z}$, the crests are closer together. 
To travel the time $\frac{2\pi}{\omega_{0}- \sqrt[]{ 1- \frac{v^{2}}{c^{2}} }}v_{z}$, so each crest has $\frac{2\pi}{\omega_{0}\sqrt[]{ 1- \frac{v^{2}}{c^{2}} }} v_{\frac{z}{c}}$ less time to propagate to the observer. The time between crests from the observer is thus
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{2\pi}{\omega_{0} \sqrt[]{ 1- \frac{v^{2}}{c^{2}} } }\left( 1- \frac{v_{z}}{c}  \right)
\end{align}
$$

This tells us that 
$$
\begin{align}
\omega  & = \frac{2\pi}{\left( \frac{2\pi}{\omega_{0}\sqrt[]{ 1-\frac{v^{2} }{c^{2}} } } \right)\left( 1- \frac{v_{z}  }{c} \right)} \\
 & \approx \omega_{0} \left(  1+ \frac{v_{z} }{c} \right)
\end{align}
$$

We have a Boltzmann probability distribution in the form
$Ae^{\frac{E}{k_{B}T}}$

$$
\begin{align}
P(v_{z} ) = \frac{1}{\sqrt[]{ 2\pi \left( \frac{k_{B}T}{m}  \right) } } e^{\frac{-v_{z}^{2} }{2 \frac{k_{B}t }{m}}}
\end{align}
$$

## Pressure Broadening

When atoms collide with each other (close neighborhood), they change the potential and broaden the atomic transitions. At each collision they add a random phase shift, so the coherence time is far smaller.
