
- What are some of the practical problems with solid-state batteries? What do the authors claim lead to these problems?
Dendrites grow between the solid state electrodes at either side of the batteries, eventually causing short circuit. This is largely due to poor contact from low wettability of the solid state electrodes, which  causes lithium ion flux -> dendrite growth and short circui.

- What is interfacial compatibility? And how does it relate to wettability?

The contact between Lithium and the Solid State Electrodes depends on the roughness of the surface and how tightly bound the two grains are. If they do not always have faces meeting (i.e. gaps form), then other chemistry is able to form within the gaps. Wettability is how well the lithium spreads across the surface and forms a good contact - if we have a ball of lithium, only the bottom is really in contact - and the rest is exposed. If lithium pulls strongly onto the material, than that large surface is covered (high wettability -> better surface contact -> good interfacial compatibility).


- How does the garnet electrolyte stop lithium dendrites (chemically? mechanically?)

The main goals were to reduce lithiophobic species, to have more homogenous lithium flux, and lower vacancy concentration along the surface (less are for dendrites to form).

The garnet preferred a cubic organization, which had a low vacancy concentration - this was a physical/ mechanical way of preventing the dendrites. 

They also had a postproduction treatment to prevent the formation $Li_{2}CO_{3}$ and $LiOH$ from reduced moisture reactivity at the grain boundary (so a chemical thing that yielded a physical reduction of vacancies).
